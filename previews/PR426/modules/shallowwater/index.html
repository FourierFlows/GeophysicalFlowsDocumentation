ShallowWater

Basic Equations

This module solves the two-dimensional rotating shallow water equations for a fluid layer over bottom bathymetry:

\[\begin{aligned} \partial_t u + \bm{u \cdot \nabla} u - f v & = - g \partial_x \eta , \\ \partial_t v + \bm{u \cdot \nabla} v + f u & = - g \partial_y \eta , \\ \partial_t h + \bm{\nabla \cdot} (\bm{u} h) & = 0 , \end{aligned}\]

where $\bm{u}(\bm{x}, t) = (u, v)$ is the horizontal fluid velocity, $h(\bm{x}, t)$ is the total fluid depth, $f$ is the Coriolis parameter, and $g$ is the gravitational acceleration. The free-surface elevation $\eta(\bm{x}, t)$ and the bottom bathymetry $b(\bm{x})$ are both measured from the rest height of the free surface, $z = 0$. Thus,

\[\eta(\bm{x}, t) = h(\bm{x}, t) + b(\bm{x}) .\]

For example, a flat bottom at depth $H$ corresponds to $b = -H$ and then, at rest, $h = H$.

The module evolves the equations in conservative form, using the volume fluxes $q_u \equiv h u$ and $q_v \equiv h v$ and the depth $h$ as variables:

\[\begin{aligned} \partial_t q_u + \partial_x \left( \frac{q_u^2}{h} \right) + \partial_y \left( \frac{q_u q_v}{h} \right) - f q_v & = - g h \partial_x \eta + \mathcal{D} q_u + F_{q_u} , \\ \partial_t q_v + \partial_x \left( \frac{q_u q_v}{h} \right) + \partial_y \left( \frac{q_v^2}{h} \right) + f q_u & = - g h \partial_y \eta + \mathcal{D} q_v + F_{q_v} , \\ \partial_t h + \partial_x q_u + \partial_y q_v & = \mathcal{D} h + F_h , \end{aligned}\]

where $\mathcal{D} = -\nu (-\nabla^2)^{n_\nu}$ is a (hyper)-viscous operator and $F_{q_u}$, $F_{q_v}$, $F_h$ are optional forcing terms.

The pressure-gradient term is written as $- g h \bm{\nabla} \eta$. For a flat bottom, this is equivalent to the divergence form $- g \bm{\nabla} (h^2/2)$. With non-trivial bathymetry, $- g h \bm{\nabla} \eta = - g \bm{\nabla} (h^2/2) - g h \bm{\nabla} b$. Writing it as $- g h \bm{\nabla} \eta$ has a numerical benefit: a lake at rest, that is, $\bm{u} = 0$ and $\eta =$ constant, is an exact steady solution of the discrete equations, because the spectral derivative of a constant $\eta$ vanishes identically. (For that to hold, the bathymetry is dealiased; see Params.)

The (hyper)-viscous operator acts on each of $q_u$, $q_v$, and $h$. Since it vanishes for the domain-averaged components, dissipation conserves the total mass $\int h \, 𝖽x 𝖽y$ and the total volume fluxes $\int q_u \, 𝖽x 𝖽y$ and $\int q_v \, 𝖽x 𝖽y$.

The depth $h$ must remain positive everywhere; the equations are singular when $h = 0$, so wetting and drying are not supported.

In the absence of dissipation and forcing, the equations conserve the total mass and the total energy

\[E = \int \left[ \tfrac1{2} h (u^2 + v^2) + \tfrac1{2} g \eta^2 \right] \frac{𝖽x 𝖽y}{L_x L_y} .\]

Implementation

The state variable sol is a three-dimensional array of size (nkr, nl, 3) that holds the Fourier transforms of $q_u$, $q_v$, and $h$; that is, sol[:, :, 1], sol[:, :, 2], and sol[:, :, 3] correspond to $\widehat{q_u}$, $\widehat{q_v}$, and $\widehat{h}$ respectively. The equations are time-stepped forward in Fourier space:

\[\begin{aligned} \partial_t \widehat{q_u} & = - i k_x \widehat{q_u q_u / h} - i k_y \widehat{q_u q_v / h} + f \widehat{q_v} - g \widehat{h \partial_x \eta} - \nu |𝐤|^{2 n_\nu} \widehat{q_u} + \widehat{F_{q_u}} , \\ \partial_t \widehat{q_v} & = - i k_x \widehat{q_u q_v / h} - i k_y \widehat{q_v q_v / h} - f \widehat{q_u} - g \widehat{h \partial_y \eta} - \nu |𝐤|^{2 n_\nu} \widehat{q_v} + \widehat{F_{q_v}} , \\ \partial_t \widehat{h} & = - i k_x \widehat{q_u} - i k_y \widehat{q_v} - \nu |𝐤|^{2 n_\nu} \widehat{h} + \widehat{F_h} . \end{aligned}\]

Products are computed in physical space and are dealiased.

The initial condition is prescribed in terms of the primitive variables $u$, $v$, and $h$ via set_uvh!.

Parameters and Variables

All required parameters are included inside Params and all module variables are included inside Vars.

For the decaying case (no forcing, $F = 0$), vars can be constructed with DecayingVars. For the forced case ($F \ne 0$) the vars struct is with ForcedVars or StochasticForcedVars. The forcing array Fh has the same size as sol, i.e., it holds the forcing for each of $q_u$, $q_v$, and $h$.
